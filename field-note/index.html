Field note · 22 November 2026

Two teams showed me a policy. Neither showed me evidence.

By Elena Brandt

I asked three teams building image generators the same question last week: show me the evidence that your outputs are marked under Article 50(2). Two sent me a PDF policy. One sent a screenshot of a settings toggle. Not one of them could show me a dated verify log from the actual pipeline.

That is the gap I want to write about, because on 2 December the grace period for systems already on the market runs out. Eleven days, at the time I am writing this.

The date is real, and it is not where people look

Article 50's transparency duties applied from 2 August 2026. The extra time for older systems is not inside Article 50 at all — it was added as a new paragraph 4 to Article 111 by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. The operative sentence is short: providers of systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026 must take the necessary steps to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026. I checked it against the EUR-Lex text rather than a summary, because most summaries get the article number wrong.

If your system launched after 2 August, there is no transitional period. You were already on the clock.

A policy is a statement of intent. Evidence is a test result.

Those are different objects, and only one of them can be checked by somebody who does not trust you. A policy says what you intend to do. Evidence says: this file, on this date, produced this result, and here is the command that reproduces it. An auditor cannot run a PDF. They can run a log.

So this is the list I now ask teams to put in front of me. It fits on one page and none of it is expensive.

  • A role map. For every feature that emits synthetic output, write down who is the provider and who is the deployer. If you wrap, fine-tune or resell someone else's model, you are very likely a provider of the wrapped system. This is the mistake that changes which duty lands on you.
  • A marking spec. Say which mechanism you use where: embedded metadata where you control the file, a hash-bound sidecar where you do not. "We use C2PA" or "we embed XMP" is a mechanism, not a spec, until you say which outputs it covers.
  • A dated verify log. From the pipeline, not retyped into a document. Date, file, command, exit code. The boring parts are what make it credible.
  • A survival result. Re-encode, resize, thumbnail, repost — then verify again. Write down what happened, including the failures.

The result that changed my mind about metadata. I marked a PNG correctly, with an XMP packet carrying the IPTC digital source type, and the verify step passed. Then a thumbnailer rewrote the file the way a CMS or a social platform does. The picture was pixel-identical. The mark was gone. "We embed metadata" is not an answer to "survived what?" — and if you have not run that test yet, you do not yet know what your evidence says.

What I do about it

I produce a one-pipeline Article 50(2) Evidence Report: a dated document with each finding mapped to the four statutory properties — effective, interoperable, robust, reliable — plus a reproducible method note so the result can be re-run after a model or prompt change. It is the artifact I wish those three teams had been able to send me.
