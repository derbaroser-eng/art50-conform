← Article 50(2) Evidence Report

Your EU AI Act mark is in the file. It's not in the pipeline.

The transition window for Article 50(2) machine-readable marking closes on 2 December 2026. Eight days from where I'm writing this.

I spent last week poking at my own image pipeline, and the thing that surprised me wasn't the marking itself. It was how many places the mark quietly disappears.

First, the rule. Article 50(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 says providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text content must mark the output in a machine-readable format, and that the marking must be effective, interoperable, robust and reliable, as far as technically feasible. That last phrase is doing a lot of work. It is why a policy document is not evidence.

So I went looking for evidence. Here is what I actually found in my own pipeline.

I embed an IPTC digitalSourceType XMP block — trainedAlgorithmicMedia — directly in the file. For JPEG that's an APP1 segment; for PNG it's an iTXt chunk named XML:com.adobe.xmp. The reader checks the same bytes the writer puts in, so there's no "trust me" step.

Then I hash the file and write a sidecar: sha256, source type, generator version. The sidecar is the part that survives, because it doesn't depend on any one image codec.

Here's the bit that got me. Re-encode the image once, normally, and the XMP block is gone. The file still looks identical. On a 64x64 PNG I went from 974 bytes back to 161, and the verifier flipped from "marked" to "not marked." No error. No warning. Just a smaller file.

That's the whole problem in one sentence: the mark was effective at the moment I wrote it and not robust one step later.

I don't think that means the rule is impossible. I think it means the check has to happen at the end of the pipeline, on the bytes you actually ship — not at the moment you generate them.

Three things I now do before I call a pipeline done:

  1. Check the final bytes, not the in-memory object. Whatever your CDN or export step emits is the thing that matters.
  2. Keep a sidecar with a sha256 so you can prove which bytes you checked.
  3. Re-run the check after every model or prompt change, because "as far as technically feasible" moves when the pipeline moves.

None of this is legal advice. I'm an engineer, not a lawyer. But "we mark our outputs" is not the same as "here is the marked file, here is its hash, and here is the check re-run after the last deploy."
