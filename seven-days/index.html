Seven days to 2 December: do you actually need an Article 50(2) evidence report?

By Elena Brandt · engineering evidence, not legal advice · 6 minute read

I have spent the last few weeks writing about the machine-readable marking duty in Article 50(2) of the EU AI Act. The question I get most is not about the technology. It is: do I need to pay for this at all?

Fair question. Here is the decision note I would send a colleague, with the exact legal text, a real measurement I ran this morning, and the cases where my answer is "no, you do not need it."

The date, from the source rather than a blog post

Article 50 transparency obligations applied from 2 August 2026. Then the Digital Omnibus, formally Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, added a paragraph 4 to Article 111. It reads:

Providers of AI systems, including general-purpose AI systems, generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, that have been placed on the market before 2 August 2026 shall take the necessary steps in order to comply with Article 50(2) by 2 December 2026.

Two things people get wrong. First, this is a provider duty about machine-readable marking, not the deployer disclosure duty in Article 50(4). Second, the grace period is only for systems placed on the market before 2 August 2026. If yours went out on or after that date, there was no extra window. The Commission's AI Act Service Desk says the same thing in plainer words.

What "robust" looked like on my own machine this morning

Article 50(2) says outputs must be marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated or manipulated, with the marking effective, interoperable, robust and reliable as far as technically feasible. Rather than argue about the adjectives, I took a 64×64 PNG and ran the marking tool I publish in the open:

$ python marking.py mark  p.png  m.png
mark exit 0  MARKED  (p.png 179 bytes -> m.png 992 bytes)

$ python marking.py verify m.png
exit 0  MARKED (embedded-xmp)

$ python marking.py verify p.png
exit 1  NOT MARKED (embedded-xmp)

So the mark is there and the verifier finds it. That is the easy half. The hard half is what happens on the way through a CMS, a chat app or an image optimiser, because the requirement is about the output as it leaves the pipeline, not the moment before. In an earlier run, a routine re-encode removed a mark of the same kind without anything adversarial happening. That gap is the whole reason these checks need to be written down rather than assumed.

The source is public if you want to run it yourself: art50-marking on GitHub. I would rather you check your own file first than take my word for it.

The three questions I actually ask

  1. Can you point to a marked output and a verifier result, both dated? If not, you have a policy, not evidence. That is the gap worth closing before you spend anything.
  2. Does the mark survive the ordinary path your file takes? Re-encode it the way your CMS or CDN does. If it disappears, you have a real finding, and you can fix it yourself.
  3. Is somebody outside your team going to ask you for this in writing? A regulator, a customer's procurement team, a lender. If yes, the dated and reproducible record is what you are really buying.

If your pipeline is simple, the mark survives, and nobody needs a written record, stop here. You do not need my report. I mean that.
