Before you buy: seven honest answers about the Article 50(2) evidence report

Elena Brandt · 7 minute read

I would rather answer these before you pay than after. The report costs EUR 490 once. That is real money, and if it is the wrong tool for your situation I would rather say so here.

1. Is this legal advice?

No. It is engineering evidence. I test one pipeline against the four properties in Article 50(2) and I attach the statutory wording to every finding. I am not a lawyer, this creates no client relationship, and it is not a conformity certificate. Your legal conclusion still belongs with qualified counsel.

2. What do I actually receive?

One dated report for one pipeline. It covers your synthetic audio, image, video or text output, maps each finding to the verbatim statutory text, scores the four properties — effective, interoperable, robust, reliable — and includes a method note so you can re-run the checks after a model or prompt change. One pipeline per report.

3. Why EUR 490 and not a subscription?

Because the work is a dated snapshot, not a service that keeps costing me something. There is no seat count, no renewal, no trial that turns into a bill. You pay once for one report on one pipeline. If you change the pipeline materially, that is a new report.

4. Will this make us compliant?

No, and I would distrust anyone who promises that. What it does is answer a narrower question with evidence: does the marking you claim actually survive the ordinary things that happen to a file on the way out? That is the part an auditor can test, and it is the part most policy documents do not address.

5. What does the test actually look like?

Here is a real run from this morning, not an illustration. I made a 64×64 image and embedded an IPTC XMP block describing it as algorithmically created:

plain PNG                  186 bytes   mark: no
embedded XMP               570 bytes   mark: yes
after one normal re-encode 186 bytes   mark: no

One ordinary re-encode took the file back to its original size and the marking information was gone. Nothing exotic happened to it. That gap is the thing the report measures, property by property.

6. Is the deadline real, and where does it come from?

Article 50 transparency duties applied from 2 August 2026. Systems placed on the market before that date have a transitional window ending 2 December 2026 for the machine-readable marking duty under Article 50(2), inserted by Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, Article 111(4). Systems placed on the market on or after 2 August 2026 get no grace period. I checked the Commission text rather than a summary: AI Act Service Desk, Article 50. That is eight days away as I write this.

7. What if I just want to check one file myself?

Do that first. The field note sets out the four checks in plain language and the repository has the source. If your pipeline is simple and the mark survives, you may not need a report at all. The report is for when you need the result written down, dated, and mapped to the regulation because somebody else is going to ask you about it.
